> Evidence gathered up to 15 November 2026. Facts, interpretation and gaps are kept apart.

## 1. Outreach to clubs (the owner's own emails, October–November 2026)

| Figure | Result |
|---|---|
| Emails sent | 46, in four batches |
| Replies from a person | 4 organisations, all sports clubs in the Groningen area |
| Replies from Spain | 0 of about 25 |
| Bounced | 3 |
| Replies from independent tennis coaches | 0 |

What was said (paraphrased, names left out on purpose):

- **Multi-sport club, floorball section (board member).** They tried the club's app and everyone, including
  the young coaches, went back to WhatsApp: logging in first loses most people, and some forgot their login.
  WhatsApp phone numbers are always current; nobody checks details in a club app. They use an
  announcements-only group plus one group per team. Missing in WhatsApp: a place to keep documents (referee
  rules, competition schedule, useful links) that can still be found months later, and a separate way to sign
  a child off a training. On the old demo: "looks nice, but a lot; day to day only a few things are needed".
- **Same club, board secretary.** "We already use the club app; we are covered."
- **Student handball club.** Uses PlayerPlus, including fines. The old demo's idea was "a bit too advanced for
  our league: we don't have videos or very specific practice".
- **Football club, head of youth development.** Tried the demo at once and asked whether everyone creates
  their own account and whether the goal was collecting documents. Conversation still open.
- **Student basketball club.** Misunderstood the email; no feedback yet.

**Limits:** four replies are not a market. Nobody was asked about paying. No coach has used anything for real.

## 2. Competitors

The official sites could not be opened from the research environment; the table comes from search results
that quote them. Prices marked * are from third-party listings and must be checked before they are used.

| Product | Free | Paid | How it earns |
|---|---|---|---|
| TeamPlus (SpielerPlus, PlayerPlus) | Most features, with ads | about 54 € per team per year*; volume discounts for clubs | Ads and Premium |
| Spond | Everything | No subscription confirmed | Fee on payments (about 2.5% + 0.20 € in the euro zone*) |
| Heja | Schedule, availability, messages, documents; one admin; ads | Team Pro about 100 $ per team per year; Pro Max about 140 $* | Ads and per-team subscription |
| Teamy | Basic version | Team plan from about 50 € per year*; club plan | Subscriptions, ad removal |
| SportMember | Basic plan | Pro about 0.16 £ per user per month, minimum 22 £ per month (vendor's own page) | Subscription and payment fee |
| TeamSnap | Limited | No current public prices found | Subscription and payment fee |

**TeamPlus features** (the owner's reference): events (trainings, matches, tournaments) with confirm/decline
and push notifications; line-ups with templates; tasks; team treasury with a fines catalogue; statistics;
team chat; polls; lifts; a private file store; parent and deputy accounts; roles (coach, caregiver, treasurer,
player); iOS, Android and web. Premium adds: no ads, custom roles, guest players, calendar subscription,
planned absences, PDF export, automatic notifications about answers.

**How people join competitors:** in Spond a new member receives an email, downloads the app, creates an
account and accepts. Children are added without contact details; guardians answer for them and the last
answer counts.

## 3. Interpretation

- The category is mature and nearly free for the team. Price per team is low, so revenue needs volume.
- TeamPlus already has documents and absences, the two things missed in WhatsApp. The gap is the effort of
  getting in, not missing features.
- A product that only copies TeamPlus gives nobody a reason to switch. Lower friction and no ads are the
  candidate differences; neither is proven.

## 4. Gaps

- No independent study was found on why clubs return to WhatsApp; only vendor blogs.
- No willingness-to-pay signal.
- Free-tier limits of TeamPlus (for example a 30-member cap) are reported inconsistently.
